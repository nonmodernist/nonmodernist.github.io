"unhappy dangerous boy"

The Damned opens on the Weymouth esplanade with a gang of teddy boys whistling a pop song whose whole lyric is, basically, "black leather, black leather, smash smash smash." Their leader, King, is not in leather. He wears a tweed jacket and carries a furled umbrella like a guards officer. He is Oliver Reed in his early twenties, using his sister as bait to roll an American tourist.

That's the first reel. Then Joseph Losey walks the film off the promenade and down the cliff into something else entirely: a sculptor's studio on the headland (Viveca Lindfors, working among Elisabeth Frink's real bronzes), a civil servant with a secret, and a classroom of polite children whose skin is cold to the touch.

Reed's King is all appetite and jealousy, too fond of his sister by far, but for once the picture around him is bigger and colder than he is. Nobody has to pretend he is a light comedian (https://letterboxd.com/nonmodernist/film/take-a-girl-like-you-1970/); the film simply has something more frightening in the basement. It is also the least Hammer of Hammer films, moreso even than the next year's Paranoiac: black-and-white 'Scope, shot on the Dorset coast, structured like a trapdoor instead of a corridor (https://letterboxd.com/nonmodernist/film/the-curse-of-the-werewolf/).
